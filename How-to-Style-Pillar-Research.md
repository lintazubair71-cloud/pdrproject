## 0. The one fact that drives all four answers

The live SERPs for our actual sub-topic keywords (checked: `how to style purple jeans men`, `how to style khaki shorts women`) are **not** owned by big fashion authorities. They are dominated by AI-spun / expired-domain spam (langmai.org, quantaservices subdomains, databee, 500apps, etc.) plus a few mid-tier blogs. This matches the roadmap's thesis: **KD ≈ 0, REAL difficulty low**. 

That means our competition is of **two different kinds**, and we beat each one differently:
- For the **sub-topics**: we beat thin AI spam on *quality* (real photos, 15 distinct looks, shoes named, internal links). Easy.
- For the **pillar term `how to style clothes`**: we compete with real editorial hubs on *structure and topical depth*. Harder — the pillar page has to look like a genuine, well-linked table of contents for a real cluster, not a thin category archive.

## 1. Competitors

> Live fetch of competitor sites was blocked by this environment's network policy (thetrendspotter.net, thevou.com did not resolve). The landscape below is assembled from the search SERPs that *did* return and from the established players in this exact niche. Treat specific page structures as "verify when you can browse them," but the tiering and the strategic read are solid.

### Tier A — Direct format competitors ("how to style X: N outfits" listicle hubs)
These do exactly what we do: image-led, numbered-outfit listicles, both genders, Pinterest-fed. **This is who we are really fighting for the sub-topic keywords.**

| Competitor | What they do | Why we can beat them |
|---|---|---|
| **The Trend Spotter** (thetrendspotter.net) | Huge library of "how to wear / outfit ideas" listicles, men + women, strong Pinterest presence | Older, broad but shallow per post; we go deeper (15+ named looks, consistent model, shoes visible) |
| **The VOU** (thevou.com) | "Style" mega-hub, aesthetics + decades + how-to, long listicles | Very wide, sometimes thin on a given long-tail; we own the exact long-tail with a cleaner page |

| **Glowsly, Her Style Code, Kembeo, Fashionterest** | Women-led outfit listicles | Thin, templated; our overlap-checked cluster signals more expertise |
| **Next Luxury / men's listicle sites** | Men's "how to wear" listicles | Men's long-tail is under-served; roadmap deliberately over-indexes here |

### Tier B — Authority editorial (own the *head* term, not our long-tails)
Rank for broad `how to style` / `outfit ideas`, strong brand + backlinks. We do **not** fight them head-on early; we take the long-tail they don't bother with.

- **Who What Wear** — trend-led, polished, shopping-linked (women).
- **Byrdie / InStyle / StyleCaster / Cosmopolitan** — editorial outfit ideas, big DA.
- **He Spoke Style, Gentleman's Gazette, Permanent Style, Effortless Gent** — men's style, formula-driven.
- **Fashion Jackson** — minimalist capsule/outfit angle (women).

### Tier C — Educational / "develop your style" hubs (different intent)
- **The Concept Wardrobe** (theconceptwardrobe.com) — teaches *method*: build a wardrobe, develop personal style, care for clothes. Its homepage is a clean 3-section hub. **Relevant as a structural model for our pillar page** (a teaching intro + organised links), even though their article intent differs from our listicles.

### Tier D — Retail / brand blogs
- Mizzen+Main, Windsor, Gant, SheerLuxe, Petite Dressing, etc. — product-led styling posts. They convert but read as commerce; we win on editorial neutrality and breadth.

### Competitive takeaways for the pillar
1. **Nobody in Tier A/B has a tight, overlap-checked, 30-link `how to style` pillar for *both* genders at our quality bar.** That gap is our wedge.
2. **Spam is beatable on-page**; authority is beatable on long-tail selection. Our keyword list is already chosen for that.
3. **Pinterest is the traffic on-ramp** for Tier A — the roadmap already treats the keyword set as a Pinterest export. The pillar page is the *destination* that captures and routes that traffic.
4. **Design + image consistency is our visible differentiator.** Most Tier A/C competitors mix stock photos; CLAUDE.md's "identical wall/floor/light within a guide, shoes always visible" rule is a real on-page quality edge.

## 2. Pillar page structure (`/fashion/how-to-style/`)

Goal of the page: (a) rank for `how to style clothes`, (b) be the hub that links **down** to all 30 sub-topics so Google reads a real cluster, (c) route Pinterest/long-tail visitors deeper. Per roadmap: *publish the pillar first, short; fill it out properly once sub-topics are live.*

Recommended structure, top to bottom:

1. **H1 + intro (100–200 words).** H1 = *How to Style Anything: The Complete Guide* (or close). First ~150 words naturally use "how to style clothes" and state what the hub covers: men's and women's styling, organised by garment, colour, and piece. One sentence that frames the method ("every guide gives you 15 real outfits, head to toe, shoes included").
2. **How this guide works / the method (short).** 3–5 principles (fit first, build around one piece, keep it to real looks you can copy). This is the "expertise" signal the Concept Wardrobe model does well. Keep it skimmable.
3. **Browse by audience — Women / Men / Unisex.** Primary navigation split. Mirrors the roadmap's audience tagging and matches how competitors segment.
4. **Browse by type (sub-sections / H2s), each with a card grid linking down to the relevant sub-topics.** Suggested groupings that map cleanly onto the 30 (and keep internal anchors natural):
   - **Trousers & jeans** — grey/white/beige/brown/green pants, purple/stacked/cargo jeans, grey sweatpants, leather pants
   - **Suits & tailoring** — white suit (M/W), brown suit, linen suit
   - **Shorts & skirts** — khaki shorts, khaki cargo pants, leather skirt
   - **Tops & dresses** — crochet tops, princess/lavender/casual/denim dresses, men in dresses, pink button-up
   - **Outerwear** — leather jacket, varsity jacket
   - **Shoes** — chunky shoes, red bottom shoes, white loafers
   - *(Grouping is for UX + anchor variety only — it does not change URLs; all stay flat at `/fashion/how-to-style/<slug>/`.)*
5. **Featured / start-here row** (3–6 highest-volume or best guides, e.g. purple jeans men 17k, casual dress for men, denim outfits for women). Gives the page a strong first screen and spreads link equity to money pages later.
6. **Short FAQ (optional, 3–4 Q's).** "How do I start styling an outfit?", "What should every outfit include?" — natural place for the head keyword + related terms, and earns PAA/snippet real estate.
7. **Affiliate disclosure** above the first affiliate link (per CLAUDE.md), not footer — relevant once product links appear.
8. **Footer link back up to `/fashion/`** (category) and across to the other 6 pillars.

**Length at launch:** short is fine (roadmap says so). **Length when mature:** enough intro + method + FAQ to stand as content (~600–900 words of text) wrapped around the 30 links. It should never be *only* a bare archive grid.

**On-page SEO notes:**
- Title ≤ 60 chars; `how to style clothes` in H1, title, first 150 words, and one H2.
- Permalink one level deep: `/fashion/how-to-style/` (no dates) — already set.
- The pillar must link to **all 30** sub-topics (no orphans). This is the single most important on-page job of the page.

## 3. Pillar vs sub-topic boundary (how overlap is avoided)

The roadmap already solved this systematically; the rules below are the ones to enforce when writing.

### The core rule
**The pillar owns the topic broadly; each sub-topic takes one specific garment/angle.** The pillar page targets the *head term* `how to style clothes`. **No sub-topic is allowed to target that head term** — the roadmap explicitly "reserves" pillar head terms. Each of the 30 targets its own narrow primary keyword (e.g. `khaki shorts women`, `purple jeans men`), one keyword per article, set by hand.

### How "no overlap" was enforced (from the roadmap, keep applying it)
1. **Content signature per keyword.** Normalise gender words, strip filler ("outfit", "ideas", "best", "style"), normalise plurals/apostrophes so `90s` = `90's`.
2. **Conflict test.** If two articles' signatures are a subset of each other, or ≥60% identical → conflict → only one survives.
3. **Checked both inside the pillar and across all 7 pillars.** So e.g. `how to style summer dresses` lives in **Seasonal**, not here, even though it starts with "how to style" — because its signature is seasonal. This is the kind of near-miss to watch.
4. **Result claimed:** 0 overlapping pairs across 120 articles, 0 sub-topic colliding with a pillar.

### Boundary rules to apply as you write the "How to Style" cluster
- **Pillar page:** broad, evergreen, teaches the method + indexes the 30. Does **not** go deep on any single garment. Does **not** target a sub-topic keyword.
- **Each sub-topic:** one garment/colour/piece, one primary keyword, 15+ distinct outfits for *that* piece. Does **not** restate the general "how to style anything" advice beyond a one-line intro; links up to the pillar for that.
- **Watch these specific near-collisions:**
  - `how to style a leather jacket` (here) vs `varsity jacket women` (here) — both outerwear, but different garments → fine; link as siblings via "jacket".
  - `men in dresses` (here) vs `casual dress for men` (here) vs `princess/lavender dresses` (here) — all "dress" but different garment/intent → fine; siblings via "dress".
  - `how to style summer dresses` is **Seasonal**, not here. Do not create a duplicate here.
  - Colour pants (grey/white/beige/brown/green) are separate articles, each its own colour keyword — not one merged "coloured pants" article. Keep them separate; they'll inter-link via "pant".
- **One keyword = one article.** Never let a second article chase a keyword another already owns (roadmap keeps a 1:1 keyword→article map in Tab 6).

## 4. Internal linking plan

The roadmap defines the full plan (Tab 5). Summary + the 30-row map for this pillar below.

### The link shapes (apply to every article)
1. **UP (sub-topic → pillar):** every sub-topic links to `/fashion/how-to-style/` **once**, inside the first 150 words, anchor = `how to style clothes`. One link, not three.
2. **DOWN (pillar → sub-topics):** the pillar links to **all 30** sub-topics. This is the link that actually builds the cluster — any guide the pillar doesn't link to is an orphan.
3. **SIDEWAYS (sub-topic → 2–3 siblings in the same pillar):** linked on a genuine shared term (khaki, pant, jean, suit, white, dresse, shoe, grey, jacket, leather). Where two same-pillar articles share no term, link them **via the pillar instead** — do not force a sideways link.
4. **ACROSS (cross-pillar):** only where a shared term makes it natural (e.g. "white", "dresse", "shoe", "casual", "tops", "how"). If the plan says "do not force one," add none. A forced cross-link is worse than no link. ~43 of 120 articles have one.
5. **CATEGORY:** `/fashion/` links to all 7 pillars; all 7 link back to `/fashion/`.
